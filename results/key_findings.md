# Key findings (generated from the analysis output)

- 1000 students; no missing values or duplicates; one score of 0 and a few low outliers were kept and treated as valid scores.
- Average score overall: 67.8. 103 students (10.3%) have an average below 50 ('at risk' in this project).
- Test preparation: students who completed the course scored 7.6 points higher on average (72.7 vs 65.0; Cohen's d = 0.56; p < 0.001).
- Lunch type: students with standard lunch scored 8.6 points higher on average than free/reduced lunch (d = 0.62; p < 0.001).
- Parental education: the mean average score is lowest for 'high school' (63.1) and highest for 'master's degree' (73.6); Kruskal-Wallis H = 44.7, p < 0.001; the overall trend is positive but modest (Spearman rho = 0.19).
- Gender: girls scored 7.1 points higher in reading and 9.2 higher in writing; boys scored 5.1 points higher in math.
- Reading and writing scores are very strongly correlated (r = 0.95); math is also strongly correlated with both (r = 0.82 and 0.80).
- These are associations, not proof of cause. For example, students who chose the prep course may differ in other ways.
